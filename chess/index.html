Patch notes v1.6.1

v1.6.1 Update

  • Players elo is no longer stored in the players browser.

v1.6 Spectating

  • 📺 Watch live games: see every online game being played right now and press Spectate to watch one move by move, with both players, their Elo and the move timer. Watching never changes your Elo.
  • When someone is watching your game, their name shows above the board.

v1.5.3 Move timer

  • 1:30 to move: in online games you have a minute and a half to make each move. The timer is shown above the board and turns red in the last 15 seconds. If it runs out, you lose the game on time.

v1.5.2 Bug fixes and improvements

  • Bug fixes and improvements.

v1.5.1 Elo reset and quick match alerts

  • Everyone starts at 2000 Elo. Every rating was reset to 2000, and new players start there too. There is no starting level to pick any more.
  • Your Elo can drop all the way to 0 if you lose, and there is no top: win and it keeps climbing past 2000.
  • Leaderboard reset: everyone who has played is still on it, all on 2000 and sharing first place until they play. It now ranks by your Elo right now, not your best.
  • Quick match alerts: when someone is waiting for a quick match, everyone else with Chess open gets a pop-up asking if they want to join. One tap on Join and the game starts.

v1.5 Local 2-player and a leaderboard

  • Local 2-player: under Opponent, pick Local 2-player to play a friend on the same computer. After White moves, the board turns round so Black is at the bottom, and back again after Black moves. Undo takes back one move. These games don’t count towards your rating or record.
  • Leaderboard: press 🏆 Chess leaderboard to see everyone with an Arcade account who has played chess, ranked by their highest online rating.
  • Fixed the patch notes: the ratings update is now listed as v1.4, which it was (it was still showing as v1.3).

v1.4 Ratings

  • Online games are rated, the way chess.com does it (Glicko). The first time you play online, pick how well you play (400 to 2000) to set your starting rating.
  • Your rating and your opponent’s show next to the names. After each game you see how much it went up or down, and your best rating is kept.
  • Only online games against a person count. A game where someone leaves before both sides have moved is aborted and not rated. Leaving a game you’ve both moved in counts as a loss.

v1.3 Six levels

  • Three new computer levels on top of the old ones, so there's one that fits how you play: Casual (between Easy and Medium), Advanced (between Medium and Hard) and Master (harder than Hard: it thinks for 4 seconds and looks deeper).
  • Your record is kept for each of the six levels.

v1.2 Chat

  • Chat with your opponent in online games: type in the box under Play online and press Enter.

v1.1 Play online

  • Play another person online: type your name under Play online and press Quick match to play whoever else is waiting.
  • Or create a private table and send a friend its 5-letter code; they type it in and press Join with code.
  • The table's maker plays White first, and you swap colours every rematch. Press Rematch after a game to play again.
  • Resign gives up the game; leaving the table counts as a loss. Undo is off online.
  • Your online record is kept on its own, and your game against the computer is still there when you leave the table.

v1.0 First release

  • Play chess against the computer on a classic green board, seen from above.
  • Three computer levels: Easy, Medium and Hard.
  • Every rule of chess: castling, en passant, promotion, check, checkmate, stalemate, and draws by repetition, the 50-move rule and not enough pieces to mate.
  • Drag a piece or click it, then click where it should go. Legal moves are shown as dots.
  • Play as White, Black or a random colour. Undo, flip the board, resign, and a move list.
  • Your game is saved, so you can close the page and carry on later. Your wins, losses and draws are kept for each level.

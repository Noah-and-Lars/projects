# Project Fish

A fast-paced multiplayer first-person shooter for the browser, built from scratch with [three.js](https://threejs.org).

**Status: phase 1.** A flat grey baseplate where you can look around, walk and jump, with a small house in front of you so you can see yourself moving. You play as a soldier carrying five pistols (the PF-9, the M1911, the Beretta 92FS, the FN 502 and the Browning Hi-Power, on keys 1 to 5), which shoot, reload and can be inspected; you can change his outfit and the PF-9's attachments in the loadout menu (B). The arms have weight: they trail behind turns, lean into strafes, bounce with your steps, dip when you land, and pull the gun back when you walk up to a wall. The map and multiplayer come later.

## Play it locally

The game is split into several files, so the browser has to load it from a web server rather than straight from disk. From the repository root:

```bash
python3 -m http.server 8000
```

Then open <http://localhost:8000/project-fish/> in Chrome, Edge, Firefox or Safari. You need an internet connection, because three.js loads from a CDN.

Click to lock the mouse to the game. Press Esc to get the mouse back.

## Controls

| Key | Action |
|---|---|
| WASD | Move |
| Shift (hold) | Sprint (going forwards); double-tap for a tactical sprint (faster, while stamina lasts) |
| Mouse | Look |
| Space | Jump; at a ledge, climb onto it (or vault over it if it's low and thin); stand up |
| C | Crouch (toggle); while sprinting, slide (double-tap: a longer slide onto your back) |
| Z | Go prone (toggle); while sprinting, dive |
| Left mouse | Fire |
| Right mouse (hold) | Aim down the iron sights |
| Q | Aim: click to toggle, or hold to aim until you let go |
| R | Reload |
| F | Inspect the gun (not the FN 502 or the Hi-Power, which have no inspect) |
| 1 to 5, mouse wheel | Switch guns: the PF-9, the M1911, the Beretta 92FS, the FN 502 and the Browning Hi-Power |
| B | Loadout menu: choose each gun's attachments, and your outfit (B or Esc closes it) |
| T | Switch the gun's light and laser on and off (when it has them) |
| P | Photo mode: fly round and look at yourself (P again to go back) |
| Esc | Release the mouse |

The top-left corner shows the frame rate, your speed, whether you're on the ground or in the air, your stance or move, and your stamina.

Everything in `src/shared/` is plain JavaScript with no three.js or browser code. When multiplayer is added, the server will run the same movement code, so players move identically on the server and in the game.

The game logic runs at a fixed 60 ticks per second, whatever the screen's frame rate. The camera is drawn smoothly between ticks, and mouse look is applied every frame so aiming never lags.

## The soldier

A soldier in full tactical gear stands beside the house door, facing where you start, in his idle pose (breathing, shifting his weight). He's the character players will be when multiplayer arrives; until then he's there to look at. You can't walk through him, and shots stop on him. His model is `assets/soldier.glb` (3.2 MB), made from "Soldier Full Tactical Gear" by DanlyVostok (see Credits): 84,000 triangles with a full skeleton (fingers, face and twist bones) and its idle animation. Where he stands is `SOLDIER` in `src/shared/map.js`. The world lights him with the same outdoor sky reflections the gun uses, so his dark olive kit reads in daylight.

`tools/soldier/build_soldier.mjs` makes the file from the download: it rubs the lettering off his chest and back patches and blanks the round badge on his helmet (`tools/soldier/remove_patch_text.py`), drops texture coordinates no material uses, shrinks the textures from 4096 px to 1024 (the colour and normal maps; the rest to 512) as WebP, and compresses the meshes. At 1024 px he looks nearly the same as with the full 4K textures at any distance you'd see him from, and takes a quarter of the graphics memory of 2048 px. To rebuild it, download the model's glTF version from Sketchfab, unzip it into `build/soldier-source` (so the folder holds `scene.gltf`, `scene.bin` and `textures/`) and run `npm run build-soldier` (it needs Python 3 with numpy and Pillow).

## Your own body

You are the soldier too: look down and you see his legs, boots and kit, and the sun casts his whole shadow (`src/player/body.js`, a copy of the same model). The camera sits just behind his eyes, so his head, helmet, glasses and eyes are left out of the picture (they still cast their shadow).

His own arms hold the gun (`src/player/fpArms.js`): his sleeves and gloves are drawn in the gun layer from the same model file and geometry, with their own copy of his skeleton. That copy's chest is held where his chest is seen from the camera standing still and looking straight ahead, so the arms reach the gun the same way however his body moves as he walks or runs and however far up you look; looking down, it goes over to where his body's chest really is, so the sleeves meet his shoulders. His body in the world gets the same arm pose from its own shoulders, for its shadow. The hands are still animated by the gun layer's hand rigs (every grip, reload and inspect), and each frame his hand is put where the rig's is (his middle knuckle on its middle knuckle), each finger bone turned as the rig's, and his upper arm and forearm reach it from his shoulder by two-bone IK, the elbow hanging down and out, with the lower forearm taking half the wrist's twist. His gloved hands are thicker than the rigs', so they're fitted to the gun: each gun's shape is made once (and again for each set of attachments) as a distance field of its solid parts on a 3 mm grid (`src/player/gunVolume.js`), a hand whose palm is in it moves out, and each finger bone in it, knuckle to tip, turns about its joint out to the surface (the thumbs sideways, along the frame). If his arms can't reach (looking up, mostly), the whole gun is drawn in towards his shoulders, eased and at most 0.35 m/s so it never jumps, so the hands never leave it. To reach, the gun is held 0.4 m from the camera with a wider view of the gun layer (62°), so it's the size on screen it was further out. `settings.soldierArms` (in `src/settings.js`) switches back to the separate gloves model, which is also used if the soldier model can't be loaded. He faces where you look, leans a little with the view, and his neck and head take the rest of it. His hips move so his eyes stay just in front of the camera, sliding back as you look down so you look down past his chest, and his feet stay planted (two-bone leg IK, `src/player/ik.js`). Walking and running, his feet take turns: each is planted and goes back under him at his speed (so it stays put on the ground), then swings forward, lifted, as many steps a metre as the gun's bob so they land with its footstep jolts; his hips dip at each step, and in the air his feet tuck up. Crouching lowers his hips and bends his knees. Sliding, he sits back low with his lead leg out ahead and the other bent, his body a little further back so you look down at his legs. Prone, diving and climbing aren't posed yet, so his body is left out then (his arms still hold the gun).

## Your outfit

The loadout menu (B) has an Outfit tab: a picture of your soldier in what he's wearing, standing in his idle pose and slowly turning (drag to turn him yourself; Full, Head and Gloves frame him head to toe or close up; in the Gloves view he swings to and fro instead, so his right hand stays in view), beside the slots and their options. Choosing one puts it on him straight away, in the picture, on your own body (its shadow, looking down, photo mode) and on the soldier by the house, and it's remembered in the browser.

| Slot | Options |
|---|---|
| Head | Field helmet (his own, with ear defenders built in), Comms helmet (a high-cut helmet with rails, ear cups and a boom mic), Headset (ear defenders and a boom mic, no helmet), None (just his balaclava; his glasses come off too, as their arms were made to tuck under his helmet's ear cups) |
| Vest | Field carrier (his own plate carrier and the pouches on it), Black carrier (triple magazine pouches and a flag patch), Sage carrier (heavier, sage grey, with webbing all round), None (just his jacket; his belt and its pouches stay) |
| Gloves | No gloves (the hands the Beretta, the FN 502 and the Hi-Power came with; he keeps his field gloves on, as the soldier model has no bare hands under them), Field gloves (his own, with a hard knuckle guard; he starts in these), Black gloves (a padded knuckle guard, knuckle pads and a wide wrist strap), Sage gloves (worn sage green, with moulded knuckles and a long strapped cuff), Camo gloves (a hard knuckle guard and a tan leather palm) |

The pieces that aren't his are made from downloaded models by Exactly (see Credits) and fitted to him at real size (`assets/gear/*.glb`, 0.45 to 1.8 MB each, loaded when first worn or shown). Head gear sits where his own helmet sits (the headset over his ears, its band drawn down to rest on his head), clear of his face and glasses, and moves with his head; in first person it's left out of the picture like his head, but casts its shadow. A vest is scaled, along each axis, to where his own plate carrier is, pushed out wherever it would cut into his jacket, and skinned to his spine and collarbones with the weights of the nearest points of his own plate carrier, so it moves with him as he breathes, leans, crouches and slides. Wearing one hides his own piece for that slot (`OWN` in `src/gear.js` says which of his meshes they are).

The gloves are worn on every pair of his hands: your arms holding the gun (every grip, reload and inspect), your body (its shadow and photo mode), the soldier by the house and the menu's picture. Each download is a static glove lying flat with its fingers out, so it's rigged to his hand bones (`tools/gear/glove.mjs`): its joints are found from its own shape (its fingertips from its outline, each finger's middle line followed down from its tip slice by slice, the knuckles a little below the webs between the fingers, and the finger joints spaced as his), it's turned and scaled so its knuckles lie on his, and each point is weighted to the finger or thumb it's on (shared only with the digits it's joined to over the glove's surface, so fingers that touch don't drag each other) and to the bones either side of each joint, blended across it. His hand skeleton is posed to match the glove, and the glove is stretched a little along each bone so its joints sit exactly on his; then it's bent half way towards his rest pose (as rigid turns, so the bends keep their volume) and bound there, so his bones bend it the rest of the way into whatever his hands do. The thumb's root is his. Its cuff goes up into his sleeve far enough to leave no gap (drawn out if it's short), narrowed wherever it would poke through the sleeve, and follows his lower forearm as his own glove's does. The left glove of each download is used for both hands, mirrored for the right. The big ones are simplified only after they're rigged, keeping the rings of points each joint bends at (the camo pair from 59,000 triangles to 12,000, the sage pair from 20,500 to 12,000). The black pair is low-poly (4,000 triangles), so it's first subdivided once into a smooth surface (`tools/gear/subdivide.mjs`; from the hand up, as its wrist strap is a layer over the cuff) and then simplified the same way to 12,000, so its fingers bend round instead of folding into slivers. The sage gloves' woven fabric is so fine that, shrunk, it shimmered in swirls and stripes up close: its colour texture is blurred and calmed first and tinted sage. The camo gloves' joints blend over a wider stretch on the fingers' palm side (`palmBlend`), so tight curls crumple its palm leather less. Each file also says how thick its fingers and palm are to his own gloves', and the first-person arms scale the fit to the gun (`FIT` in `src/player/fpArms.js`) by it.

The Beretta, the FN 502 and the Hi-Power bring their own arms, and those wear the gloves too: the black, sage and camo gloves were also fitted to each gun's own hands (`assets/gear/gloves_<id>_beretta.glb`, `gloves_<id>_fn502.glb` and `gloves_<id>_hipistol.glb`, 0.6 to 2.7 MB each: 2048 px colour textures, and 2048 px normal maps on the black ones and 1024 px on the camo ones). With No gloves or Field gloves they keep the hands they came with (bare on the Beretta, its own leather gloves on the FN 502, its own black gloves on the Hi-Power), since his field gloves were never made for them. Each is rigged the same way as on him (`tools/gear/glove.mjs`), only to the gun's own hand and finger bones in the pose its arms were skinned in, by `tools/gear/gun_gloves.mjs`; its cuff takes the weights of the gun's own forearm where it lies (widened clear of the Beretta's bare skin, narrowed into the FN 502's and the Hi-Power's sleeves), and the file says where to cut the gun's own hand away under it. In the game (`src/viewmodel/gunGloves.js`) the glove is only skinned to the gun's bones, found by name, so the gun's own clips move it exactly as they move its hands: nothing is posed on top of them. The gun's arm mesh gets a second list of triangles without the hand (made once for each glove, over the same points, so going back to its own hands just uses the first list again). A gun is dressed when it's taken out (its glove loads while the other gun is put away) and again when the gloves are changed; a new glove goes on, and its hand comes off, in one frame once its file is in and its materials are ready, so the bare hand never shows in between, and a gun out of view takes its old glove off at once. To rebuild them, from the project-fish folder after the guns are built: `node tools/gear/gun_gloves.mjs build/gear-src assets/beretta.glb assets/fn502.glb assets/hipistol.glb assets/gear`. (The Hi-Power's arms are two meshes, its gloves and its sleeves: the gloves are the ones cut away under the new glove, and the sleeves are read beside them for the cuff to fit inside; its Rigify hands' bones are named in the script's `RIGS`, as their finger bones hang under bones in the palm that carry no weight.)

The slots and options are `SLOTS` in `src/gear.js`. Each copy of the soldier (the one by the house, your body, your arms holding the gun, the menu's picture) is dressed by the `Wardrobe` there, which puts each piece on that copy's own bones (each file carries a copy of his skeleton, so its joints are found by name). Another option is one more entry in `SLOTS` with its `file`; gloves tagged for first person are also drawn in the gun layer on your arms. The menu's picture has its own small renderer and only draws while it's on screen.

`tools/gear/build_gear.mjs` makes the files from the downloads (the gloves with `tools/gear/glove.mjs`, as above): it checks that each download's `scene.gltf` names only files in its own folder, simplifies the big meshes (the comms helmet from 19,000 triangles to 14,000, the sage carrier from 21,000 to 12,000), turns, scales and places each piece on the soldier, pushes it clear of him (and draws the headset's band down onto his head), skins it, shrinks its texture (2048 px for the comms helmet and the black and camo gloves, 1024 for the rest, and the gloves' normal maps to 1024) as WebP, compresses the mesh and keeps the credit in the file (`asset.copyright`). `tools/gear/rub_headset_text.py` first rubs the print (a unit name, a line of words, a bird emblem and a number) off the headset's ear cups. To rebuild them, download each model's glTF version from Sketchfab, unzip it into `build/gear-src/<folder>` (`tactical_helmet_with_headset_game-ready`, `tactical_headset_-_game_ready`, `tactical_plate_carrier_vest_-_game_ready`, `military_armor_3d_model`, `tactical_combat_gloves_-_game_ready`, `tactical_combat_gloves_3d_model` and `military_tactical_gloves_-_multicam_hard_knuckle`; each holds `scene.gltf`, `scene.bin`, `textures/` and `license.txt`) and run `npm run build-gear` (after `npm run build-soldier`, as it fits them to `assets/soldier.glb`; it needs Python 3 with numpy and Pillow). Where each piece goes and how big it is are `fit` in `ITEMS` at the top of the script; for a glove, `glove` there says which of the download's gloves to use, which way its hand faces, how high the webs between its fingers are and roughly where its thumb is.

## Moving

The movement is in `src/shared/movement.js` (shared with the future server), with its numbers in `MOVEMENT` and `PLAYER` in `src/shared/config.js`. They're modelled on how modern military shooters move:

- **Walking** at 5 m/s, a little slower backwards (0.75) and sideways (0.9). Aiming 0.6, crouched 0.5 (0.35 aiming), prone 0.9 m/s.
- **Sprinting** (hold Shift going forwards) at 6.8 m/s, as long as you like. **Tactical sprint** (double-tap Shift) at 8.4 m/s for up to 4 s of stamina: it slows towards a plain sprint as stamina runs low, and stamina comes back over 3 s after a short pause. The view widens a little while it lasts, and the pistol comes up close in front of the chest, muzzle up, swinging harder with the longer strides.
- **Crouching** (C, a toggle) and **going prone** (Z). Space stands up. You can't stand up where there's no room overhead.
- **Sliding** (C while sprinting): a burst of speed (1.2 times your sprint) that slows over 0.75 s, low enough to go under a 1 m bar. You can steer it a little, and jump out of it after 0.15 s to keep sprint speed (a slide cancel). The view rolls into it and widens, and the gun drops and cants over with a fine judder from the ground. It ends crouched. **Double-tap C** while sprinting for a longer, lower slide onto your back; getting up takes half a second.
- **Diving** (Z while sprinting): a low leap forward that lands you prone and slides you to a stop. Not straight after a jump.
- **Jumping** 1 m high. You land on top of things and walk up anything 35 cm or lower. You can still jump just after walking off an edge, and a jump pressed just before landing still happens.
- **Mantling**: Space at a ledge up to 2.1 m above your feet (or moving towards one in the air, so a jump reaches higher ledges) climbs onto it. Something up to 1.15 m high and 0.8 m thick you vault over instead. You keep your speed coming out (Mantle momentum), and C during a vault goes straight into a slide. The gun dips and rolls while you climb and can only fire if you're aiming.
- **Landing** from 1.5 m or more slows you for a moment (more from 3.5 m), less if you were sprinting.
- Shots stray less crouched (0.8) or prone (0.65) and more sliding, diving or in the air (1.5).

A small course to the left of the spawn has something for each: crates 0.5, 1 and 1.5 m high, a low wall to vault, a 2 m wall and a 2.6 m ledge to climb (jump first for the ledge), a bar to slide under, and three steps (`OBSTACLES` in `src/shared/map.js`).

## Tuning movement

All movement values are in `src/shared/config.js`:

| Value | Now | Meaning |
|---|---|---|
| `walkSpeed` | 5 m/s | Top walking speed |
| `sprintSpeedFactor` | 1.36 | Sprinting speed (6.8 m/s), as a fraction of walking speed |
| `tacSprintSpeedFactor` | 1.68 | Tactical sprint (8.4 m/s); `tacSprintTime` 4 s of stamina, back over `staminaRegenTime` 3 s |
| `slideBoost`, `slideFriction`, `slideTime` | 1.2, 7 m/s², 0.75 s | A slide's burst, how fast it slows and how long it lasts at most |
| `mantleReach`, `vaultMaxHeight` | 2.1 m, 1.15 m | How high a ledge you can climb, and how high a wall you vault |

| `airAccel` | 12 m/s² | How much you can steer in the air |

## Hands and gun

The gloves and sleeves you see are a detailed model made in Blender (`assets/arms.glb`, about 1.2 MB).

The first pistol, the PF-9, is a compact polymer-frame 9 mm holding 15 rounds (`assets/pistol.glb`, about 4.8 MB with its attachments). It's made from "Pistol 9mm Custom" by ~D-Try~ (see Credits), a detailed model that comes with a set of attachments, by `tools/blender/convert_pistol9.py` and `tools/finish_pistol9.mjs`. The download is a showcase scene with four copies of the pistol and a row of loose attachments; the script takes the fitted copy with its magazine in and places it so its grip sits where the game's hands hold a pistol, 14% wider than the model (the model is real size in length and height but thinner than the real thing). It groups its pieces into the parts the game moves (`slide` with its `extractor`, `barrel` with a `chamber_round`, `frame`, `trigger` with its `safety_blade`, `slide_stop`, `mag_release`, and `magazine` with a `magazine_round`, `magazine_second_round` and `magazine_follower`, which it adds), and keeps each attachment as a hidden part named `att_<id>` in its mounted place. On the textures it rubs out the maker's and model markings, serials and emblems, engraves a made-up "PF-9" on the slide, and rubs the brand names, logos and codes off the attachments; it also shrinks them (2048 px for the pistol, 1024 px or less for the rest). Its magazine slides out along its own axis, a little steeper than the grip line the hands are posed round (`magDown`). To rebuild it, download that model's glTF version from Sketchfab, unzip it into `build/pistol9-source` (so the folder holds `scene.gltf`, `scene.bin` and `textures/`) and run `npm run build-pistol`. The earlier PF-9, made entirely from scripts (`tools/blender/build_pistol.py`, from the profiles in `src/viewmodel/pistol.js`), is described below; `npm run build-pistol-own` builds it into `build/`, and a simple built-in pistol stands in if the model can't be loaded.

The second pistol, the M1911, is a full-size .45 (`assets/pistol45.glb`, about 1.5 MB). It's made from "Colt m1911" by neyr (see Credits), a detailed model with textures, by `tools/blender/convert_pistol45.py`. The script turns and scales it into gun space (216 mm long) and places it so its grip sits where the game's hands hold a pistol. It groups its pieces into the parts the game moves (`slide`, `barrel`, `frame`, `hammer`, `trigger`, `slide_stop`, `thumb_safety`, `mag_release`, `magazine` with its `magazine_round` and `magazine_follower`, and a `chamber_round` in the barrel), and makes the walnut grip panels thicker so the fingers wrap them. It also adds the middle of the barrel, seen when the slide locks back. On the textures it rubs out the maker's name, engraves just "CAL .45" in its place, and darkens the steel a little, keeping the wear on the edges. To rebuild it, download that model's glTF version from Sketchfab, unzip it into `build/m1911-source` (so the folder holds `scene.gltf`, `scene.bin` and `textures/`) and run `npm run build-pistol45`. To try other sizes or holds, the script also takes `--grow K` (K times bigger, about the middle of the grip) and `--raise D` (D metres higher in the hand). An earlier 1911-style pistol made entirely from scripts (`tools/blender/build_pistol45.py`, from the profiles in `src/viewmodel/pistol45.js`) is kept as a fallback; `npm run build-pistol45-own` builds it into `build/`.

The third pistol, the Beretta 92FS, is a full-size 9 mm holding 15 rounds, and it comes with its own bare arms and its own animations (`assets/beretta.glb`, about 1.1 MB). It's made from "Beretta Pistol FPS ANIMATION" by BURNER (see Credits); the arms are a Mixamo stock character's. It's a 92FS by its shape: an open-topped slide, the safety and decocker lever on the slide, a recurved trigger guard and no rail under the barrel (so not the railed 92A1, M9A1 or 92X). While it's held, the soldier's arms aren't drawn in the gun layer (the outfit's gloves are worn on the gun's own hands instead, see Your outfit); his body and its shadow stay, and in photo mode (and in the shadow) his body's hands take the hold the gun's own hands have in its clips: each hand turned as its hand is, his fingers pointing as its fingers do, his arm reaching there from his shoulder. The gun's own arms, which you see, are never changed. It's played by `src/viewmodel/animatedGun.js` with its maker's clips as they are: nothing is posed, bent or moved on top of them. ### Aiming down the sights

Hold the right mouse button (or click Q to toggle, or hold Q) and the pistol comes up in about 0.28 s, with a slight swoop and a small settle at the end, until the line through its three sight dots runs straight through your eye: the front dot sits exactly where the centre dot was. The world zooms in a little, the centre dot fades out, and you walk at 55% speed (`aimSpeedFactor` in `src/shared/config.js`). The arm physics keeps working while you aim, so the sights trail behind turns and swing back, and they sway: slow, uneven drifts from breathing and tired arms that never quite repeat, plus a faint tremor. The sway is about 2.4 times worse just after the sights come up and calms down over a second, and it's worse while you walk. The timing, distance and zoom are `AIM` in `src/viewmodel/viewmodel.js`; the wobble and how much of each movement is kept while aiming are `aiming`, `aimWobble` and `aimSettle` in `MOTION`. The sight positions are `SIGHTS` in `src/viewmodel/pistol.js`.

### Firing

The pistol is semi-automatic: one shot per click, up to 500 a minute (clicks made faster than that wait their turn for a moment instead of being lost). It holds 15 rounds with 60 in reserve; the numbers are `PISTOL` in `src/shared/config.js`, and the rules are in `src/weapon.js`.

- **Where shots go:** through the sights when aiming (so the sway matters), roughly at the centre from the hip, straying more from the hip and while moving. They hit the ground and the house (`raycastWorld` in `src/shared/map.js`) and leave a bullet hole and a puff of dust.
- **Kick:** the gun flips up and shoves back into the hands on the arm springs (`recoil` in `MOTION`), and the view kicks up and springs back with a little lasting climb you pull down against (`RECOIL` in `src/main.js`). Both are gentler while aiming.
- **The gun:** the trigger comes back and resets; the slide cycles on each shot (with a second, smaller jolt as it slams home) and locks back on the last round. The barrel is its own part: it only follows the slide a few millimetres and its back end tips down, like a real pistol's, so with the slide locked back the barrel pokes out of the front. The spent case flies out of the ejection port, spins, bounces and pings on the ground.
- **Small moving parts** (so you can see the gun's state up close): the slide stop turns up into a notch in the slide when the slide locks back on an empty magazine; the extractor stands out a little with a round in the chamber (the loaded-chamber indicator) and sits flush when the chamber is empty; the safety blade in the trigger's face presses in as the trigger is pulled; and the magazine shows what's in it: brass behind the witness holes down to the level the rounds reach, the top round and the one under it while there are rounds, and the follower when it's empty. The amounts are `SLIDE_STOP`, `INDICATOR_TRAVEL` and `BLADE_TRAVEL` in `src/viewmodel/viewmodel.js`.
- **Muzzle flash:** a brief, irregular fireball of hot gas bursting out round the front of the slide, a short flame plume and a few burning powder sparks, different every shot (now and then barely visible), lighting the hands and surroundings for an instant (`src/effects/muzzleFlash.js`).
- **Smoke:** a wisp from the muzzle on every shot. Each shot also heats the barrel, which cools over a few seconds; after a few quick shots, thin smoke keeps curling off the muzzle and out of the ejection port for a few seconds (about three after a whole magazine; `HEAT` in `src/main.js`).
- **Sound:** a 9mm report built in code sample by sample (pressure spike, bright blast, low punch, ground bounce, slide clack) with outdoor echoes, a click when the gun is empty, and reload sounds, including a dropped magazine clattering on the ground (`src/effects/audio.js`).
- **Reloading (R):** the gun comes up and in, muzzle tipped up a little so the magazine well comes into view, and the shooting hand's thumb comes down onto the magazine release and presses it. From empty, the support hand has already let go and is heading for the belt, and the empty magazine drops free; it carries on falling in the world, clatters on the ground and stays there (the last dozen are kept). With rounds left, the support hand turns palm up under the grip first, the old magazine drops into it, and it pulls it out and puts it away on the way to the belt, so the rounds left in it are kept. Either way it closes round a fresh magazine at the belt and brings it up held the way it's pushed in: palm up, baseplate in the palm, index finger along the front, the other fingers and the thumb closed on it (each closes until it touches the magazine). It lines it up under the well and pushes it straight in: the fingers slide off its front as it goes up into the grip, and the palm pushes it home (the gun jolts as it clicks in). Then the hand drops away, turns back and comes up beside the grip half open, and closes round it again; from empty, the slide is then released forward. 2.1 s with rounds left, or 2.5 s from empty. The hands' movements are `HAND_PATHS` in `src/viewmodel/viewmodel.js`, their hand shapes are in `src/viewmodel/supportHand.js` and `src/viewmodel/shootingHand.js`, and the timings are `RELOAD_STEPS` in `src/weapon.js`. The ammo counter is bottom right.

### Five guns

You carry five pistols: the PF-9 (15 rounds, quick), the M1911 (7 rounds, slower to fire and kicking harder, with a deeper shot), the Beretta 92FS (15 rounds, see above), the FN 502 (10 rounds of .22, quiet and light) and the Browning Hi-Power (13 rounds, see above). Press 1 to 5, or turn the mouse wheel, to switch: the gun in your hands drops down out of view (0.2 s) and the other comes up (0.3 s). Each keeps its own ammo. Switching puts an inspect away, and a reload too, unless the fresh magazine is already in (then it counts). A shot, reload or inspect asked for while switching happens as soon as the gun is up. The rules for each gun are `PISTOL`, `PISTOL45`, `BERETTA`, `FN502` and `HIPISTOL` in `src/shared/config.js`. (The Beretta and the Hi-Power take their own draw and hide animations' time instead: see above.)

The PF-9 and the M1911 use the same hands and inspect. The M1911 reloads its own way: it comes up turned further over towards the support hand, and from empty, after the magazine goes in, the support hand goes up and over the back of the slide, takes hold of it (palm on top, fingers over the right side), pulls it back the last bit and lets go so it slingshots home, rather than the thumb dropping the slide stop (2.9 s; its `reloadSteps` in `src/shared/config.js`, the hand's keys in `rackFromBehind` in `src/viewmodel/viewmodel.js`). The PF-9's thumb presses its slide stop. What's different on each gun, and where it is, is in `MODEL9` in `src/viewmodel/pistol9.js` and `MODEL45` in `src/viewmodel/pistol45.js` (and `POINTS9` and `FALLBACK45` for the built-in stand-ins): the muzzle and ejection port, the sight line, the slide's travel, the barrel and slide stop's pivots, the M1911's hammer and its magazine release (the shooting hand's thumb goes to whichever gun's it's holding). The M1911's hammer is cocked; it falls as the trigger breaks, the slide pushes it back, and it settles back on the sear. Its slide, magazine release and magazine sounds are recordings (`assets/sounds/`, see Credits): the slide pulled back and snapping forward, an empty magazine and one with rounds in it coming out, and a magazine pushed home.

### Inspecting

Press F to look the gun over (7.2 s). The support hand lets go and drops out of the way, and the shooting hand brings the gun up and rolls it over to show its right side (the ejection port and the loaded-chamber indicator), then turns it the other way, muzzle to the left, to show its left side (the slide stop, which is up if the slide is locked back, and the markings). It turns the gun the way a real arm does: as much as it can by twisting the forearm about its own length, so the sleeve stays where it is, and the rest by bending the wrist and turning the forearm at the elbow. Then the gun comes upright and up, as for a reload, for a magazine check: the thumb presses the release, the magazine drops into the support hand waiting palm up under the grip, and the hand pulls it out and holds it up so you can see the top round and how full it is (or the follower if it's empty). It lines it back up and pushes it home. Then it checks the chamber: the gun turns muzzle right so the top and right side of the slide face you, and the support hand takes hold of the slide over the top in front of the ejection port (palm against its left side, index and middle fingers over onto its right side, thumb along its left) and eases it back half way. The extractor draws the round in the chamber back with it, so its brass shows in the ejection port. The hand lets go and the slide snaps forward, and the hand takes hold of the gun again. With the slide locked back on an empty gun the chamber is plain to see, so that part is left out (5.8 s). The M1911's inspect lingers longer on each of the four things it shows (its right side, its left side, the magazine held up and the slide held back), so it takes 10.4 s (8.4 s without the chamber check); the moves between are as quick as the PF-9's. Each gun's extra time per hold is `inspectHolds` in `src/shared/config.js`, and the holds are `INSPECT_HOLDS` in `src/weapon.js`.

Firing, aiming, reloading or sprinting cuts it short: the gun goes back to the hold in a quarter of a second, and then does what you asked (a shot isn't lost). If the magazine is out or the slide is held back at that moment, that's finished first, four times faster. You can't start an inspect while reloading, aiming or sprinting. The gun's and arms' keyframes are `INSPECT_POSES` and the support hand's path is `HAND_PATHS.inspect`, both in `src/viewmodel/viewmodel.js`; the hand's hold on the slide is `OVER_TURN` and `OVER_SLIDE` in `src/viewmodel/supportHand.js`; the timings are `INSPECT` in `src/weapon.js`.

### Sprinting

Hold Shift while moving forwards (straight or at an angle) to sprint at 7.5 m/s, 1.5 times walking speed (`sprintSpeedFactor` in `src/shared/config.js`). The hands bend down at the wrists, tipping the gun muzzle down (the gloves' cuffs stay with the sleeves), and the crosshair fades. Sprinting needs you to be going forwards and not aiming; it starts on the ground and carries on through a jump. Aiming or firing stops it. A shot fired mid-sprint waits the moment it takes the gun to come back up (about 0.2 s), and sprinting picks up again 0.4 s after the last shot if Shift is still held. You can reload while sprinting; the gun stays in its reload pose. The pose is `sprint` in `MOTION` (`src/viewmodel/motion.js`).

### Weapon physics

The hands and gun hang on springs (`src/viewmodel/motion.js`), so they have weight. Turning makes them trail behind and swing back past centre when you stop; speeding up or stopping shoves them the other way; strafing leans them into the turn; each footstep gives a small bounce; jumping and landing make them dip and nod; breathing keeps them from ever being perfectly still. Close to a wall (a ray straight ahead hits something under 85 cm away) the gun is pulled back and tipped up. Sprinting, the hands bend down at the wrists the other way, tipping the gun muzzle down while the forearms stay put, and the longer strides swing it more. They swing about a point between the wrists, not the eye, so they move like arms. Every value is in `MOTION` at the top of the file, with a comment saying what it does.

### Rebuilding the models

The models are made by scripts, so they can be changed and rebuilt. You need [Node.js](https://nodejs.org) and [Blender](https://www.blender.org) (tested with 5.2, with `blender` on your PATH). From the `project-fish` folder:

```bash
npm install
```

```bash
npm run build-models
```

This takes a few minutes. It converts the five pistols from their downloads (`assets/pistol.glb`, `assets/pistol45.glb`, `assets/beretta.glb`, `assets/fn502.glb` and `assets/hipistol.glb`, which need their sources in `build/pistol9-source`, `build/m1911-source`, `build/beretta-source`, `build/fn502-source` and `build/hipistol-source`, see Hands and gun), the soldier (see The soldier) and his gear (see Your outfit). It exports the hand pose and pistol profiles from the game's code, builds and textures the arms in Blender (`tools/blender/build_arms.py`), compresses them, and writes `assets/arms.glb`. It also saves `build/pistol9.blend`, `build/pistol45.blend` and `build/arms.blend`, which you can open in Blender to look at or keep working on the models by hand. `npm run build-pistol`, `npm run build-pistol45`, `npm run build-beretta`, `npm run build-fn502`, `npm run build-hipistol`, `npm run build-arms`, `npm run build-soldier` and `npm run build-gear` rebuild one at a time.

If a model made by hand in Blender replaces one of these files, keep the same scale (metres) and orientation (muzzle towards -Z, Y up), and keep the pistol's parts named as they are (`slide`, `barrel`, `frame`, `trigger`, `safety_blade`, `slide_stop`, `extractor`, `magazine` and the magazine's `magazine_round`, `magazine_second_round`, `magazine_follower` and `magazine_rounds_1` to `_6`) so they animate. The slide stop and the barrel turn about fixed points (`SLIDE_STOP` and `BARREL` in `src/viewmodel/viewmodel.js`), not about their own origins, because compressing the file moves those.

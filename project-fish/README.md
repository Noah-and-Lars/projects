# Project Fish

A fast-paced multiplayer first-person shooter for the browser, built from scratch with [three.js](https://threejs.org).

**Status: phase 1.** It opens on a main menu: two soldiers on a rooftop at night, seen from behind, looking out over a city far below, with Play, the loadout, the breakdowns and the cinematic beside them (see The main menu). The game itself is a flat grey baseplate where you can look around, walk and jump, with a small house in front of you so you can see yourself moving. You play as a soldier carrying twelve guns (six pistols, the PF-9, the M1911, the Beretta 92FS, the FN 502, the Browning Hi-Power and the Xiri X4, then an MP7A1 sub-machine gun, an AR-15 rifle, a seventh pistol, the Makarov PM, on keys 1 to 9, then two assault rifles, the AK-74M and the AK-74U, and a shotgun, the Franchi SPAS-12, on 0, - and =), which shoot (the MP7A1 and the two AKs fully automatic, the SPAS-12 nine pellets a shot), reload and (all but six) can be inspected; you can pick a gun, change his outfit and the PF-9's attachments in the loadout menu (B), which also has the gun breakdowns (the AR-15, the FN SCAR-L, the IWI Galil ACE and the PF-9): a gun taken apart on screen part by part, each part labelled with what it does. The arms have weight: they trail behind turns, lean into strafes, bounce with your steps, dip when you land, and pull the gun back when you walk up to a wall. The map and multiplayer come later.

## Play it locally

The game is split into several files, so the browser has to load it from a web server rather than straight from disk. From the repository root:

```bash
python3 -m http.server 8000
```

Then open <http://localhost:8000/project-fish/> in Chrome, Edge, Firefox or Safari. You need an internet connection, because three.js loads from a CDN.

The game opens on its main menu. Play (click it, or Enter) locks the mouse to the game. Press Esc to get the mouse back: the game pauses and the menu comes back over it, with Resume at the top.

## Controls

| Key | Action |
|---|---|
| WASD | Move |
| Shift (hold) | Sprint (going forwards); double-tap for a tactical sprint (faster, while stamina lasts) |
| Mouse | Look |
| Space | Jump; at a ledge, climb onto it (or vault over it if it's low and thin); stand up |
| C | Crouch (toggle); while sprinting, slide (double-tap: a longer slide onto your back) |
| Z | Go prone (toggle); while sprinting, dive |
| Left mouse | Fire (hold it down with the MP7A1, the AK-74M and the AK-74U, which are fully automatic) |
| Right mouse (hold) | Aim down the sights (the iron sights, or the MP7A1's and the AR-15's optics) |
| Q | Aim: click to toggle, or hold to aim until you let go |
| R | Reload |
| F | Inspect the gun (not the FN 502, the Hi-Power, the AR-15, the Makarov PM, the AK-74M or the SPAS-12, which have no inspect) |
| 1 to 9, 0, - and =, mouse wheel | Switch guns: the PF-9, the M1911, the Beretta 92FS, the FN 502, the Browning Hi-Power, the Xiri X4, the MP7A1, the AR-15 and the Makarov PM on 1 to 9, the AK-74M on 0, the AK-74U on - and the Franchi SPAS-12 on = (the wheel goes round from the last to the first; the loadout menu can also put any of them in your hands) |
| B | Loadout menu: the gun in your hands (any of the twelve) and its attachments, and your outfit, on two tabs (B or Esc closes it); its "Breakdowns" button opens a picker of the gun breakdowns, and Esc there goes back (see The loadout menu and Breakdowns) |

| Esc | Release the mouse and pause: the main menu comes back over the game, with Resume at the top |

The top-left corner shows the frame rate, your speed, whether you're on the ground or in the air, your stance or move, and your stamina.

## The main menu

The game opens on its main menu (`src/menu/mainMenu.js`, its look in `src/menu/menu.css`, its words in `index.html`). The title is top left; down the left side: **Play** (into the game: the mouse locks to it, and the menu fades into the game), **Loadout** (the loadout menu, B in the game), **Breakdowns** (its picker of the gun breakdowns and the cinematic), **Cinematic** (plays the dive film), then smaller, **Controls** and **Credits** (each a panel beside the list: every key, and every model, sound and font the game uses), and in red at the bottom **Fullscreen** (where the browser has it). The version is top right: package.json's, and the day that file last changed on the server. The mouse over an item, or the arrow keys, slide a red bar to it; Enter or a click chooses it; Esc closes a panel. Until the game has loaded, Play says Loading. Opened as a file (file://) rather than from a web server, the menu says the game can't load that way.

Esc in the game (or anything else that lets the mouse go) pauses: the same menu comes back over the game, darkened, with **Resume** at the top instead of Play (just after Esc, the browser may want a second click before it gives the mouse back; the menu says so). B in the game still opens the loadout menu straight away. The loadout menu, the breakdowns and the cinematic opened from the menu come back to it when they close.

Behind the title screen is a live backdrop (`src/menu/backdrop.js`), drawn by its own renderer while the game stands still behind it: a night rooftop, its edge wet and caught by a strong red light from the right, with cool moonlight from behind; and 150 m below, a city made in code, a few thousand blocks in one instanced mesh whose shader draws their lit windows (warm, white and a few cool, offices more lit; far off, their average, so they don't shimmer), clusters of towers with blinking red aircraft lights, the streets as slow streams of car lights (white coming, red going) between rows of street lamps and a glow on the street grid, a plane crossing far off, haze, a low cloud deck lit from below, a few out-of-focus lights, light rain, film grain and a vignette. The two soldiers are copies of the game's soldier in his idle clip, out of step with each other: the one on the left wears your outfit (change it in the loadout menu and he changes at once), the one on the right a different one picked from yours. The camera drifts slowly and follows the mouse a little; the left side stays dark for the menu. On a phone it draws fewer pixels and less of the city and no rain, the camera frames them for an upright screen, and on any device it lowers its resolution if frames come too slowly. It stops drawing while the page is hidden or the loadout menu, a breakdown or the cinematic is open, and once you play it's freed (its renderer, its own geometry, materials and textures, and its listeners on the soldier's and his gear's files, which the game keeps).

## The loadout menu

The loadout menu (`src/loadoutMenu.js`, its look in `src/loadout.css`) opens with B in the game or Loadout in the main menu, and goes back to whichever it came from. It covers the whole screen, in the main menu's look: while it's open the game draws nothing behind it, and neither does the title screen's backdrop. In the middle stands your soldier, big (`src/outfitPreview.js`): a copy of the game's soldier in your outfit, in his idle clip (nothing poses him), in a dark studio: a cool key light from the front left that casts his soft shadow on the floor, a red rim light from behind on the right, a faint cool one from behind on the left, his reflections in the same colours, a darker contact shadow under his boots and a faint pool of light round him, over a dark gradient with a little film grain. Drag him to turn him; let go and after a moment he turns back to stand three-quarters on. He has his own renderer, which draws at most about 4 million pixels and only while a tab shows him.

The title is top left, with the two tabs under it, **Loadout** and **Customization** (Q and E change tab); **Breakdowns** is top right (see Breakdowns), **Back** bottom left (Esc, or B from anywhere in the menu: back to the game, or to the main menu if it was opened from there), and the keys bottom right.

- **Loadout.** On the left, under Weapons, the gun in your hands: its picture, its name, its kind and calibre. Choosing it opens a grid of all twelve (each with its picture, kind, key, calibre, magazine and fire mode); choosing one puts it in your hands and comes back. Under it, a strip of all twelve takes one straight away, and so do the guns' keys (1 to 9, 0, - and =). Then Headwear, Body armour and Hands: what he wears on each, each opening the Customization tab on it. On the right, the gun: its kind and key, its name, its picture, a line or two on what it is (`GUN_INFO` in `src/loadoutInfo.js`), and its calibre, magazine, fire mode and rate, from its rules in `src/shared/config.js` (`calibre`, `kind`, `magazine`, `reserve`, `fireMode`, `fireInterval`, `pellets`). Under that, its attachments, a row for each slot with what's on it; choosing a row opens what fits there (with what each does, and what choosing it would take off; see Attachments). The PF-9's picture shows what's on it: each attachment is drawn as a layer over the gun, where it sits with the others, and the picture eases out or in to fit them all. The guns whose attachments can't be changed yet list what's fitted to them for good instead (the MP7A1's red dot and suppressor, the AR-15's holographic sight, magnifier and suppressor, the AK-74M's muzzle brake and the AK-74U's muzzle booster).
- **Customization.** On the left, Tactical gear (head and vest) and Hands (gloves): a tile for each slot with what's on it (a slot's `group` and `title` in `SLOTS`, `src/gear.js`), and Default kit, which puts him back in what he starts in. On the right, the chosen slot: its group and name, the option's name, what it is and whether he's wearing it (with the mouse over another, or focus on it, that one's), then a grid of every option, each drawn in its own tile, the one he's wearing marked. Choosing one puts it on him at once (see Your outfit). He's framed from the knees up, a little closer for the head, and turned to show his gun hand for the gloves.

The arrow keys move round a grid and Enter chooses. On a phone he stands in the top half, the title and tabs over him, and the rest scrolls below him (on its side, he's on the left and the rest on the right).

Its pictures are `assets/loadout/thumbs/` (41 WebP files, 1 MB, made from the same models as the game, see Credits): `gun_<key>.webp` for each gun (1024×512, its right side, muzzle to the right, without the arms its file brings; a gun with its own arms is posed as its idle clip starts and turned level by its `gun` node), `pf9_kit.webp` and a layer for each PF-9 attachment in each place it can sit (`PF9_LAYERS` in `src/loadoutInfo.js`; the layers have the gun hide whatever of the attachment is behind it, which is why the laser, on its left side, barely shows), and `gear_<slot>_<id>.webp` for each outfit option (512×512: the piece alone, on him as he stands in his idle; None on his head or his vest is him without it, and No gloves the bare hands the Beretta came with, round the grip of its gun left out). All drawn in the same studio light. `tools/loadout/thumbs.html` (with `thumbs.js`) draws them in the browser; to draw them all again, from the project-fish folder: `npm run build-loadout-thumbs`, which runs `node tools/loadout/render_thumbs.mjs assets/loadout/thumbs` (add a name prefix, such as `gear_`, to draw only those). It serves the folder itself, opens the page in a headless Chromium with WebGL in software (so the same files make the same pictures anywhere) and saves what it draws; it needs `npm install` (three.js) and Playwright with its Chromium (`npm install --no-save playwright && npx playwright install chromium`, or `PLAYWRIGHT` set to an installed copy's `index.mjs`). Opened by hand (served as for playing), the page shows them all with a link to save each. A new gun or outfit option needs its pictures drawn; till then its tile shows its name on a dark card.

The game logic runs at a fixed 60 ticks per second, whatever the screen's frame rate. The camera is drawn smoothly between ticks, and mouse look is applied every frame so aiming never lags.

## The soldier

A soldier in full tactical gear stands beside the house door, facing where you start, in his idle pose (breathing, shifting his weight). He's the character players will be when multiplayer arrives; until then he's there to look at. You can't walk through him, and shots stop on him. His model is `assets/soldier.glb` (3.2 MB), made from "Soldier Full Tactical Gear" by DanlyVostok (see Credits): 84,000 triangles with a full skeleton (fingers, face and twist bones) and its idle animation. Where he stands is `SOLDIER` in `src/shared/map.js`. The world lights him with the same outdoor sky reflections the gun uses, so his dark olive kit reads in daylight.

`tools/soldier/build_soldier.mjs` makes the file from the download: it rubs the lettering off his chest and back patches and blanks the round badge on his helmet (`tools/soldier/remove_patch_text.py`), drops texture coordinates no material uses, shrinks the textures from 4096 px to 1024 (the colour and normal maps; the rest to 512) as WebP, and compresses the meshes. At 1024 px he looks nearly the same as with the full 4K textures at any distance you'd see him from, and takes a quarter of the graphics memory of 2048 px. To rebuild it, download the model's glTF version from Sketchfab, unzip it into `build/soldier-source` (so the folder holds `scene.gltf`, `scene.bin` and `textures/`) and run `npm run build-soldier` (it needs Python 3 with numpy and Pillow).

## Your own body

You are the soldier too: look down and you see his legs, boots and kit, and the sun casts his whole shadow (`src/player/body.js`, a copy of the same model). The camera sits just behind his eyes, so his head, helmet, glasses and eyes are left out of the picture (they still cast their shadow).

His own arms hold the gun (`src/player/fpArms.js`): his sleeves and gloves are drawn in the gun layer from the same model file and geometry, with their own copy of his skeleton. That copy's chest is held where his chest is seen from the camera standing still and looking straight ahead, so the arms reach the gun the same way however his body moves as he walks or runs and however far up you look; looking down, it goes over to where his body's chest really is, so the sleeves meet his shoulders. His body in the world gets the same arm pose from its own shoulders, for its shadow. The hands are still animated by the gun layer's hand rigs (every grip, reload and inspect), and each frame his hand is put where the rig's is (his middle knuckle on its middle knuckle), each finger bone turned as the rig's, and his upper arm and forearm reach it from his shoulder by two-bone IK, the elbow hanging down and out, with the lower forearm taking half the wrist's twist. His gloved hands are thicker than the rigs', so they're fitted to the gun: each gun's shape is made once (and again for each set of attachments) as a distance field of its solid parts on a 3 mm grid (`src/player/gunVolume.js`), a hand whose palm is in it moves out, and each finger bone in it, knuckle to tip, turns about its joint out to the surface (the thumbs sideways, along the frame). If his arms can't reach (looking up, mostly), the whole gun is drawn in towards his shoulders, eased and at most 0.35 m/s so it never jumps, so the hands never leave it. To reach, the gun is held 0.4 m from the camera with a wider view of the gun layer (62°), so it's the size on screen it was further out. `settings.soldierArms` (in `src/settings.js`) switches back to the separate gloves model, which is also used if the soldier model can't be loaded. He faces where you look, leans a little with the view, and his neck and head take the rest of it. His hips move so his eyes stay just in front of the camera, sliding back as you look down so you look down past his chest, and his feet stay planted (two-bone leg IK, `src/player/ik.js`). Walking and running, his feet take turns: each is planted and goes back under him at his speed (so it stays put on the ground), then swings forward, lifted, as many steps a metre as the gun's bob so they land with its footstep jolts; his hips dip at each step, and in the air his feet tuck up. Crouching lowers his hips and bends his knees. Sliding, he sits back low with his lead leg out ahead and the other bent, his body a little further back so you look down at his legs. Prone, diving and climbing aren't posed yet, so his body is left out then (his arms still hold the gun).

## Your outfit

The loadout menu (B) has a Customization tab (see The loadout menu): your soldier big in the middle in what he's wearing, standing in his idle pose (drag to turn him), the slots on the left and the chosen slot's options on the right, each drawn in its own tile. Choosing one puts it on him straight away, there, on your own body (its shadow, looking down, photo mode), on the soldier by the house, on the main menu's soldier and in the cinematic, and it's remembered in the browser.

| Slot | Options |
|---|---|
| Head | Field helmet (his own, with ear defenders built in), Comms helmet (a high-cut helmet with rails, ear cups and a boom mic), Headset (ear defenders and a boom mic, no helmet), None (just his balaclava; his glasses come off too, as their arms were made to tuck under his helmet's ear cups) |
| Vest | Field carrier (his own plate carrier and the pouches on it), Black carrier (triple magazine pouches and a flag patch), Sage carrier (heavier, sage grey, with webbing all round), None (just his jacket; his belt and its pouches stay) |
| Gloves | No gloves (the hands the Beretta, the FN 502, the Hi-Power, the Makarov PM, the AK-74M, the AK-74U and the SPAS-12 came with; he keeps his field gloves on, as the soldier model has no bare hands under them, also on the X4, the MP7A1 and the AR-15, which he holds with his own arms), Field gloves (his own, with a hard knuckle guard; he starts in these), Black gloves (a padded knuckle guard, knuckle pads and a wide wrist strap), Sage gloves (worn sage green, with moulded knuckles and a long strapped cuff), Camo gloves (a hard knuckle guard and a tan leather palm) |

The pieces that aren't his are made from downloaded models by Exactly (see Credits) and fitted to him at real size (`assets/gear/*.glb`, 0.45 to 1.8 MB each, loaded when first worn or shown). Head gear sits where his own helmet sits (the headset over his ears, its band drawn down to rest on his head), clear of his face and glasses, and moves with his head; in first person it's left out of the picture like his head, but casts its shadow. A vest is scaled, along each axis, to where his own plate carrier is, pushed out wherever it would cut into his jacket, and skinned to his spine and collarbones with the weights of the nearest points of his own plate carrier, so it moves with him as he breathes, leans, crouches and slides. Wearing one hides his own piece for that slot (`OWN` in `src/gear.js` says which of his meshes they are).

The gloves are worn on every pair of his hands: your arms holding the gun (every grip, reload and inspect), your body (its shadow and photo mode), the soldier by the house and the menu's picture. Each download is a static glove lying flat with its fingers out, so it's rigged to his hand bones (`tools/gear/glove.mjs`): its joints are found from its own shape (its fingertips from its outline, each finger's middle line followed down from its tip slice by slice, the knuckles a little below the webs between the fingers, and the finger joints spaced as his), it's turned and scaled so its knuckles lie on his, and each point is weighted to the finger or thumb it's on (shared only with the digits it's joined to over the glove's surface, so fingers that touch don't drag each other) and to the bones either side of each joint, blended across it. His hand skeleton is posed to match the glove, and the glove is stretched a little along each bone so its joints sit exactly on his; then it's bent half way towards his rest pose (as rigid turns, so the bends keep their volume) and bound there, so his bones bend it the rest of the way into whatever his hands do. The thumb's root is his. Its cuff goes up into his sleeve far enough to leave no gap (drawn out if it's short), narrowed wherever it would poke through the sleeve, and follows his lower forearm as his own glove's does. The left glove of each download is used for both hands, mirrored for the right. The Beretta, the FN 502, the Hi-Power, the Makarov PM, the AK-74M, the AK-74U and the SPAS-12 bring their own arms, and those wear the gloves too: the black, sage and camo gloves were also fitted to each gun's own hands (`assets/gear/gloves_<id>_beretta.glb`, `gloves_<id>_fn502.glb`, `gloves_<id>_hipistol.glb`, `gloves_<id>_makarov.glb`, `gloves_<id>_ak74m.glb`, `gloves_<id>_ak74u.glb` and `gloves_<id>_saps12.glb`, 0.6 to 2.8 MB each: 2048 px colour textures, and 2048 px normal maps on the black ones and 1024 px on the camo ones). (The AK-74M's arms are the FN 502's and the Makarov's maker's, fitted the same way. The loadout menu (B) has a "Breakdowns" button at its top right. It opens a picker with, first, a card for the cinematic (see The cinematic, below), then a card for each gun that has a breakdown (its picture, its name, what the film shows and its model's credit): the AR-15, the FN SCAR-L, the IWI Galil ACE and the PF-9. Click a card, or move between them with the arrow keys and press Enter; Esc (or "Back to loadout") goes back to the loadout. The main menu's Breakdowns opens the picker on its own, and Esc there goes back to the main menu. Each plays a short film, made in the game, of that gun being taken apart: the camera glides up to one part at a time, the part slides out the way it really comes out (with the pieces that come out with it), the rest of the gun dims, a line draws from the part to a card with its name and what it does, then the part slides back in and seats with a click and the camera sweeps on to the next. Threads unscrew the way they really undo. Each film ends pulled back on the whole gun with its name, the model's credit and Replay and Close buttons. The camera's longer swings round the gun take up to 2.8 s, easing in and out, so it never whips round, and on a half turn (where it passes the gun end on) it rises and pulls back to show the whole gun on the way; where a label points at two or three parts, their lines meet the card in whichever order keeps them from crossing.

- **AR-15** (13 steps, about 2 min 10 s). In the order of a field strip: the magazine, the takedown pins (the upper receiver tips open), the bolt carrier group (drawn out with the charging handle), the charging handle, the cam pin (once the firing pin has slid out of the back of the carrier), the bolt (these come out one after another and go back in the reverse order), the pistol grip, the trigger group and safety, the handguard (it slides off the front and stays out), the barrel with its nut, gas block and muzzle brake (unscrewed the way their right-hand threads undo, and laid out in a line in front of the upper), the dust cover and forward assist (the dust cover's hinge pin slides out of the front of the upper, which the handguard and barrel were in the way of), the stock and buffer tube (the castle nut and the tube unscrewed), and the iron sights.

The magazine first (it stays out till the lower comes off); then the accessories: the scope and its mount, the folding rear and front sights (they flip up while the scope is off), the suppressor, the bipod and the vertical foregrip; then the field strip: the lower receiver tips down on its front pin, then the pistol grip, the selector and trigger (the trigger lifts out of the top of the lower), the charging handle, the folding stock (it folds to the right, as the real one does), the gas regulator, the gas piston and the barrel. ## The cinematic

The first card in the Breakdowns picker (loadout menu, B, then "Breakdowns", or the main menu's Breakdowns) is a short film (the main menu's Cinematic plays it straight away) made in the game, about 48 s long: the soldier, in the outfit you've chosen, sprints across the baseplate while an enemy soldier watches from the house; a teammate behind a stack of ammunition crates throws him a PF-9; he dives, snatches it out of the air, lands, rolls behind the concrete barriers, comes up and fires. Two quick shots strike the house; the third is the bullet cam: time all but stops at the muzzle flash, the camera rides beside the 9 mm round while cards in the breakdowns' style tell what it is (9×19 mm Parabellum, a 124-grain full metal jacket bullet), how fast it spins, what the brass case does (the camera visits the gun as it's thrown out), and its speed (live, with its Mach number and distance), until it strikes the enemy's left shoulder: a puff of dust from his sleeve and a few fibres, no blood (it's a game), and a last card on the energy it brings (½ m v², about 470 J). He twists and falls in slow motion, his pistol dropping from his hand, time snaps back with a cut to a wide shot as he falls, and the film cuts back to the shooter kneeling in cover, gun still up, looking over his shoulder at the enemy lying by the house, under the title and the credits. The dive (as he leaves the ground), the grab (the slowest: the pistol comes in across its last foot and slides into his open palm as the camera swings round, snapping back to full speed as his fingers close on it), the landing, the shot and the strike are in slow motion; time eases in and out of each, never jumping.

Space pauses, the arrows skip back and on 5 s, and Esc or Close closes it and goes back to the picker; at the end, Replay or Close. Till its code is in, a dark screen says it's loading and Esc goes back; while it's open the game stands still, as for a breakdown. `?cinematic=1` in the address also opens it once the game has loaded (`?cinematic=test` with each shot's name, story time and speed on screen), and `tools/cinematic/film.html` plays it on its own page.

How it's made (`src/showcase/cinematic/`):
- **The moves are Mixamo's** (Animations from Mixamo, see Credits). Both men are copies of the soldier (`assets/soldier.glb`), and their bodies move only by Mixamo clips, retargeted onto his own skeleton: the film chooses the clips, trims them, times them (the slow motion), cross-fades from one to the next, and places and turns the whole man (so each clip's hips carry on from where the last one's were, and his feet stay on the ground). Nothing in the code poses an arm, a hand or a finger. (In the two-handed pistol clips the retarget keeps his hands together on the gun, as Mixamo's are: his proportions alone would part them; see `tools/cinematic/README.md`.) The pistol is put in his right hand bone at one fixed grip from the catch on (`GRIP` in `actor.js`: the grip in his fist, the slide and the ejection port clear above his hand; for the firing clip it's turned a degree or two so the third shot's bore points exactly at the strike); before that it's a prop in flight. Lying on the ground, each man is raised as a whole so his body and gear never go below it. His run is Sprint; the dive, snatch, landing and roll are one take, "goalkeeper diving save" from Mixamo's Soccer Game Pack (from the catch, his right fingers close round the grip by cross-fading in the finger pose of Pistol_Kneeling_Idle); kneeling in cover, gun up in both hands, is Pistol_Kneeling_Idle, whose last frame is the first of coming up, Pistol_Kneel_To_Stand; each shot is Shooting (the clip played again from near its start for the next shot). The enemy, in a different helmet, vest and gloves from yours, stands in Pistol_Idle, and Shoulder_Hit_And_Fall takes over from the moment the bullet strikes (the clip's own reaction is to a blow to the left shoulder, which is where the bullet is aimed; it's placed so his feet stay where they were). Where the joins between clips don't match, the film cuts to another camera rather than show them (into the dive, from the dive's roll to him kneeling, and from rising to firing). `tools/cinematic/README.md` has every clip, its marks and how they chain.
- `edit.js` is the edit: where things are, both men's timelines (which clip plays from when), every shot (what stretch of the story it shows, how fast, the camera's moves and lens) and the cards' facts. `film.js` plays it: like a breakdown it has its own renderer and is a function of one clock, so any moment can be shown on its own. `actor.js` is the cast and their pistols, `set.js` the set (the game's baseplate, sky, sun and house, two concrete New Jersey barriers and the crates), `round.js` the cartridge, bullet and case, `ballistics.js` the shot's numbers (with their sources), `fx.js` the flash, smoke, strikes and dust, and `sound.js` the sounds, all made in code (footfalls, the throw, the dive, the shots, slowed in slow motion, the bullet's rush, the strike, the cases and the pistol landing).
- The clips are `assets/cinematic/soldier_clips.glb` (0.8 MB): animation only, no mesh, one animation per clip by name, each with its source in its extras. `tools/cinematic/retarget_mixamo.py` makes it from the Mixamo downloads (FBX Binary, without skin, 30 fps), which go in `build/mixamo/<Name>/<Name>.fbx` (the Soccer Game Pack in `build/mixamo/Soccer_Game_Pack/files/`) and are never committed. It needs Blender (its FBX importer) and numpy. From the project-fish folder: `npm run build-cinematic-clips`, which runs `blender --background --python tools/cinematic/retarget_mixamo.py -- tools/cinematic/clips.json assets/cinematic/soldier_clips.glb`. `tools/cinematic/clips.json` lists the clips; the same downloads always give the same file. The picker's picture is `assets/cinematic/thumb.webp`, a frame of the catch.

## Moving

The movement is in `src/shared/movement.js` (shared with the future server), with its numbers in `MOVEMENT` and `PLAYER` in `src/shared/config.js`. They're modelled on how modern military shooters move:

- **Walking** at 5 m/s, a little slower backwards (0.75) and sideways (0.9). Aiming 0.6, crouched 0.5 (0.35 aiming), prone 0.9 m/s.
- **Sprinting** (hold Shift going forwards) at 6.8 m/s, as long as you like. **Tactical sprint** (double-tap Shift) at 8.4 m/s for up to 4 s of stamina: it slows towards a plain sprint as stamina runs low, and stamina comes back over 3 s after a short pause. The view widens a little while it lasts, and the pistol comes up close in front of the chest, muzzle up, swinging harder with the longer strides.
- **Crouching** (C, a toggle) and **going prone** (Z). Space stands up. You can't stand up where there's no room overhead.
- **Sliding** (C while sprinting): a burst of speed (1.2 times your sprint) that slows over 0.75 s, low enough to go under a 1 m bar. You can steer it a little, and jump out of it after 0.15 s to keep sprint speed (a slide cancel). The view rolls into it and widens, and the gun drops and cants over with a fine judder from the ground. It ends crouched. **Double-tap C** while sprinting for a longer, lower slide onto your back; getting up takes half a second.
- **Diving** (Z while sprinting): a low leap forward that lands you prone and slides you to a stop. Not straight after a jump.
- **Jumping** 1 m high. You land on top of things and walk up anything 35 cm or lower. You can still jump just after walking off an edge, and a jump pressed just before landing still happens.
- **Mantling**: Space at a ledge up to 2.1 m above your feet (or moving towards one in the air, so a jump reaches higher ledges) climbs onto it. Something up to 1.15 m high and 0.8 m thick you vault over instead. You keep your speed coming out (Mantle momentum), and C during a vault goes straight into a slide. The gun dips and rolls while you climb and can only fire if you're aiming.
- **Landing** from 1.5 m or more slows you for a moment (more from 3.5 m), less if you were sprinting.
- Shots stray less crouched (0.8) or prone (0.65) and more sliding, diving or in the air (1.5).

A small course to the left of the spawn has something for each: crates 0.5, 1 and 1.5 m high, a low wall to vault, a 2 m wall and a 2.6 m ledge to climb (jump first for the ledge), a bar to slide under, and three steps (`OBSTACLES` in `src/shared/map.js`).

## Tuning movement

All movement values are in `src/shared/config.js`:

| Value | Now | Meaning |
|---|---|---|
| `walkSpeed` | 5 m/s | Top walking speed |
| `sprintSpeedFactor` | 1.36 | Sprinting speed (6.8 m/s), as a fraction of walking speed |
| `tacSprintSpeedFactor` | 1.68 | Tactical sprint (8.4 m/s); `tacSprintTime` 4 s of stamina, back over `staminaRegenTime` 3 s |
| `slideBoost`, `slideFriction`, `slideTime` | 1.2, 7 m/s², 0.75 s | A slide's burst, how fast it slows and how long it lasts at most |
| `mantleReach`, `vaultMaxHeight` | 2.1 m, 1.15 m | How high a ledge you can climb, and how high a wall you vault |
| `groundAccel` | 50 m/s² | How fast you reach full speed or change direction (full speed in 0.1 s) |
| `groundDecel` | 40 m/s² | How fast you stop when you let go (about 0.13 s) |
| `airAccel` | 12 m/s² | How much you can steer in the air |
| `gravity` | 20 m/s² | Stronger than real life, for crisp jumps |
| `jumpHeight` | 1 m | Height of a jump |
| `jumpBuffer` | 0.1 s | A jump pressed this long before landing still happens |

Mouse sensitivity and field of view are in `src/settings.js`.

## Hands and gun

The gloves and sleeves you see are a detailed model made in Blender (`assets/arms.glb`, about 1.2 MB).

The first pistol, the PF-9, is a compact polymer-frame 9 mm holding 15 rounds (`assets/pistol.glb`, about 4.8 MB with its attachments). It's made from "Pistol 9mm Custom" by ~D-Try~ (see Credits), a detailed model that comes with a set of attachments, by `tools/blender/convert_pistol9.py` and `tools/finish_pistol9.mjs`. The download is a showcase scene with four copies of the pistol and a row of loose attachments; the script takes the fitted copy with its magazine in and places it so its grip sits where the game's hands hold a pistol, 14% wider than the model (the model is real size in length and height but thinner than the real thing). It groups its pieces into the parts the game moves (`slide` with its `extractor`, `barrel` with a `chamber_round`, `frame`, `trigger` with its `safety_blade`, `slide_stop`, `mag_release`, and `magazine` with a `magazine_round`, `magazine_second_round` and `magazine_follower`, which it adds), and keeps each attachment as a hidden part named `att_<id>` in its mounted place. ### Aiming down the sights

Hold the right mouse button (or click Q to toggle, or hold Q) and the pistol comes up in about 0.28 s, with a slight swoop and a small settle at the end, until the line through its three sight dots runs straight through your eye: the front dot sits exactly where the centre dot was. The world zooms in a little, the centre dot fades out, and you walk at 55% speed (`aimSpeedFactor` in `src/shared/config.js`). The arm physics keeps working while you aim, so the sights trail behind turns and swing back, and they sway: slow, uneven drifts from breathing and tired arms that never quite repeat, plus a faint tremor. The sway is about 2.4 times worse just after the sights come up and calms down over a second, and it's worse while you walk. The timing, distance and zoom are `AIM` in `src/viewmodel/viewmodel.js`; the wobble and how much of each movement is kept while aiming are `aiming`, `aimWobble` and `aimSettle` in `MOTION`. The sight positions are `SIGHTS` in `src/viewmodel/pistol.js`.

### Firing

The pistol is semi-automatic: one shot per click, up to 500 a minute (clicks made faster than that wait their turn for a moment instead of being lost). (The MP7A1, the AK-74M and the AK-74U are fully automatic: they fire as long as the button is held, see Hands and gun; a gun's `fireMode` in `src/shared/config.js`. The SPAS-12 fires several pellets a shot: a gun's `pellets` and `pelletSpread`.) It holds 15 rounds with 60 in reserve; the numbers are `PISTOL` in `src/shared/config.js`, and the rules are in `src/weapon.js`.

- **Where shots go:** through the sights when aiming (so the sway matters), roughly at the centre from the hip, straying more from the hip and while moving. They hit the ground and the house (`raycastWorld` in `src/shared/map.js`) and leave a bullet hole and a puff of dust.
- **Kick:** the gun flips up and shoves back into the hands on the arm springs (`recoil` in `MOTION`), and the view kicks up and springs back with a little lasting climb you pull down against (`RECOIL` in `src/main.js`). Both are gentler while aiming.
- **The gun:** the trigger comes back and resets; the slide cycles on each shot (with a second, smaller jolt as it slams home) and locks back on the last round. The barrel is its own part: it only follows the slide a few millimetres and its back end tips down, like a real pistol's, so with the slide locked back the barrel pokes out of the front. The spent case flies out of the ejection port, spins, bounces and pings on the ground.
- **Small moving parts** (so you can see the gun's state up close): the slide stop turns up into a notch in the slide when the slide locks back on an empty magazine; the extractor stands out a little with a round in the chamber (the loaded-chamber indicator) and sits flush when the chamber is empty; the safety blade in the trigger's face presses in as the trigger is pulled; and the magazine shows what's in it: brass behind the witness holes down to the level the rounds reach, the top round and the one under it while there are rounds, and the follower when it's empty. The amounts are `SLIDE_STOP`, `INDICATOR_TRAVEL` and `BLADE_TRAVEL` in `src/viewmodel/viewmodel.js`.
- **Muzzle flash:** a brief, irregular fireball of hot gas bursting out round the front of the slide, a short flame plume and a few burning powder sparks, different every shot (now and then barely visible), lighting the hands and surroundings for an instant (`src/effects/muzzleFlash.js`).
- **Smoke:** a wisp from the muzzle on every shot. Each shot also heats the barrel, which cools over a few seconds; after a few quick shots, thin smoke keeps curling off the muzzle and out of the ejection port for a few seconds (about three after a whole magazine; `HEAT` in `src/main.js`).
- **Sound:** a 9mm report built in code sample by sample (pressure spike, bright blast, low punch, ground bounce, slide clack) with outdoor echoes, a click when the gun is empty, and reload sounds, including a dropped magazine clattering on the ground (`src/effects/audio.js`).
- **Reloading (R):** the gun comes up and in, muzzle tipped up a little so the magazine well comes into view, and the shooting hand's thumb comes down onto the magazine release and presses it. From empty, the support hand has already let go and is heading for the belt, and the empty magazine drops free; it carries on falling in the world, clatters on the ground and stays there (the last dozen are kept). With rounds left, the support hand turns palm up under the grip first, the old magazine drops into it, and it pulls it out and puts it away on the way to the belt, so the rounds left in it are kept. Either way it closes round a fresh magazine at the belt and brings it up held the way it's pushed in: palm up, baseplate in the palm, index finger along the front, the other fingers and the thumb closed on it (each closes until it touches the magazine). It lines it up under the well and pushes it straight in: the fingers slide off its front as it goes up into the grip, and the palm pushes it home (the gun jolts as it clicks in). Then the hand drops away, turns back and comes up beside the grip half open, and closes round it again; from empty, the slide is then released forward. 2.1 s with rounds left, or 2.5 s from empty. The hands' movements are `HAND_PATHS` in `src/viewmodel/viewmodel.js`, their hand shapes are in `src/viewmodel/supportHand.js` and `src/viewmodel/shootingHand.js`, and the timings are `RELOAD_STEPS` in `src/weapon.js`. The ammo counter is bottom right.

### Adding or removing a gun

A gun is a few entries that point at each other, by its key (a short id such as `ak74m`):

- **Its rules:** an object in `src/shared/config.js` (name as the game shows it, `fireMode`, `fireInterval`, `magazine`, `reserve`, reload times and `reloadSteps`, `spread`, `recoil`, `burstKick`, `pellets` and `pelletSpread`, `caseSize`, `inspect`...), listed in `GUNS` under its key, and its key in `LOADOUT`, whose order is the order of the keys (1 to 9, 0, - and =: `GUN_KEYS` in `src/main.js`, and `KEY_NAMES` in `src/loadoutMenu.js` for the menu) and of the mouse wheel.
- **Its model:** its file in `assets/`, and an entry in `GUN_KINDS` in `src/viewmodel/viewmodel.js`: the file, its rules, and either its points (a built gun, as the PF-9's) or, for a gun with its own animated arms, its `MODEL_...` constants (a file in `src/viewmodel/` naming its clips, bones, meshes, muzzle, ejection port, sights and clip events) and how it's held (`rig`, see `RIG` there: where at the hip, `eyeRelief`, `aimLevel`, `sprintPose`...), with its stand-in's rules (`standIn`).
- **Its sounds:** its shot in `GUNSHOT` in `src/effects/audio.js` (a rate and volume for the 9 mm recording, or a set of its own made in code), and its magazine and slide sounds (`RECORDED`, `LIGHT_SLIDE`).
- **Gloves** (a gun with its own arms): a `RIGS` entry in `tools/gear/gun_gloves.mjs`, its file added to that script's command (`build-gun-gloves` in `package.json`), and its key in `OWN_ARMS` in `src/gear.js`.
- **The loadout menu:** its `calibre` and `kind` in its rules, a line on what it is in `GUN_INFO` (`src/loadoutInfo.js`), and its picture (`npm run build-loadout-thumbs`, see The loadout menu).
- **Text:** the main menu's Controls and Credits panels (`index.html`, with `data-gun-name` on its name so it shows the config's), this README (controls, the gun's section, Credits), and a `build-...` script in `package.json` (added to `build-models`).

To remove one, take its key out of `LOADOUT` and `GUNS` (it's no longer carried, and nothing else asks for it), then its `GUN_KINDS` entry, its sounds, its gloves (`OWN_ARMS`, `RIGS`, the script's command and its `assets/gear/gloves_*_<key>.glb` files), its files in `assets/` and `src/viewmodel/`, its build script and its text. A saved loadout naming a gun that's gone is simply ignored.

### Attachments

Press B for the loadout menu, where the gun in your hands shows its attachments, a row for each slot (only the PF-9 has attachments so far; see The loadout menu). It lets go of the mouse while it's open; B, Esc or "Back to game" closes it. Your choice is remembered in the browser. The PF-9's come with its model:

| Slot | Choices | What they do |
|---|---|---|
| Sight | Red dot | You aim through the red dot instead of the iron sights. It needs a mount: the clamp mount (it sits over the slide and stays still) or the rail cover (it moves with the slide). |
| Mount | Clamp mount, rail cover | The clamp mount clamps to the frame's rail and bridges over the slide, with rails on top, both sides and underneath. The rail cover covers the front of the slide, with a rail on top. Only one at a time. |
| Muzzle | Short, long or box suppressor | Quieter shots that barely echo, and a much smaller flash, from the front of the suppressor. The box suppressor clamps to the frame's rail, so it can't go on with the clamp mount, and has a rail of its own underneath. |
| Under the barrel | Light, compact light | A light that shines where the gun points. It goes on the clamp mount's bottom rail, the box suppressor's rail, or the frame's own rail. |
| Side | Laser | A red dot where the gun points. It needs the clamp mount (it sits on its left rail). |

T switches the light and laser on and off: the support hand's index finger reaches forward and presses the switch (the laser's at its back corner, or the light's at its back). Choosing something that needs a mount brings one along, and choosing something that can't go on with what's on takes that off (the menu says what it would take off when you point at a choice). With the clamp mount or the box suppressor on, the inspect leaves out the chamber check, because they're where the hand takes hold of the slide; with the rail cover on, the hand takes hold of the cover. The choices and their rules are in `src/attachments.js`, what each one changes (the aim line, the muzzle, where the light and laser shine from) is in `src/viewmodel/pistol9.js`, and the viewmodel's `setAttachments` puts them on. In daylight the light only shows up close and in shade.

### Inspecting

Press F to look the gun over (7.2 s). The support hand lets go and drops out of the way, and the shooting hand brings the gun up and rolls it over to show its right side (the ejection port and the loaded-chamber indicator), then turns it the other way, muzzle to the left, to show its left side (the slide stop, which is up if the slide is locked back, and the markings). It turns the gun the way a real arm does: as much as it can by twisting the forearm about its own length, so the sleeve stays where it is, and the rest by bending the wrist and turning the forearm at the elbow. Then the gun comes upright and up, as for a reload, for a magazine check: the thumb presses the release, the magazine drops into the support hand waiting palm up under the grip, and the hand pulls it out and holds it up so you can see the top round and how full it is (or the follower if it's empty). It lines it back up and pushes it home. Then it checks the chamber: the gun turns muzzle right so the top and right side of the slide face you, and the support hand takes hold of the slide over the top in front of the ejection port (palm against its left side, index and middle fingers over onto its right side, thumb along its left) and eases it back half way. The extractor draws the round in the chamber back with it, so its brass shows in the ejection port. The hand lets go and the slide snaps forward, and the hand takes hold of the gun again. With the slide locked back on an empty gun the chamber is plain to see, so that part is left out (5.8 s). The M1911's inspect lingers longer on each of the four things it shows (its right side, its left side, the magazine held up and the slide held back), so it takes 10.4 s (8.4 s without the chamber check); the moves between are as quick as the PF-9's. Each gun's extra time per hold is `inspectHolds` in `src/shared/config.js`, and the holds are `INSPECT_HOLDS` in `src/weapon.js`.

Firing, aiming, reloading or sprinting cuts it short: the gun goes back to the hold in a quarter of a second, and then does what you asked (a shot isn't lost). If the magazine is out or the slide is held back at that moment, that's finished first, four times faster. You can't start an inspect while reloading, aiming or sprinting. The gun's and arms' keyframes are `INSPECT_POSES` and the support hand's path is `HAND_PATHS.inspect`, both in `src/viewmodel/viewmodel.js`; the hand's hold on the slide is `OVER_TURN` and `OVER_SLIDE` in `src/viewmodel/supportHand.js`; the timings are `INSPECT` in `src/weapon.js`.

### Sprinting

Hold Shift while moving forwards (straight or at an angle) to sprint at 7.5 m/s, 1.5 times walking speed (`sprintSpeedFactor` in `src/shared/config.js`). The hands bend down at the wrists, tipping the gun muzzle down (the gloves' cuffs stay with the sleeves), and the crosshair fades. Sprinting needs you to be going forwards and not aiming; it starts on the ground and carries on through a jump. Aiming or firing stops it. A shot fired mid-sprint waits the moment it takes the gun to come back up (about 0.2 s), and sprinting picks up again 0.4 s after the last shot if Shift is still held. You can reload while sprinting; the gun stays in its reload pose. The pose is `sprint` in `MOTION` (`src/viewmodel/motion.js`).

### Weapon physics

The hands and gun hang on springs (`src/viewmodel/motion.js`), so they have weight. Turning makes them trail behind and swing back past centre when you stop; speeding up or stopping shoves them the other way; strafing leans them into the turn; each footstep gives a small bounce; jumping and landing make them dip and nod; breathing keeps them from ever being perfectly still. Close to a wall (a ray straight ahead hits something under 85 cm away) the gun is pulled back and tipped up. Sprinting, the hands bend down at the wrists the other way, tipping the gun muzzle down while the forearms stay put, and the longer strides swing it more. They swing about a point between the wrists, not the eye, so they move like arms. Every value is in `MOTION` at the top of the file, with a comment saying what it does.

### Rebuilding the models

The models are made by scripts, so they can be changed and rebuilt. You need [Node.js](https://nodejs.org) and [Blender](https://www.blender.org) (tested with 5.2, with `blender` on your PATH). From the `project-fish` folder:

```bash
npm install
```

```bash
npm run build-models
```

This takes a few minutes. It converts the six pistols and the guns with their own arms from their downloads (`assets/pistol.glb`, `assets/pistol45.glb`, `assets/beretta.glb`, `assets/fn502.glb`, `assets/hipistol.glb`, `assets/makarov.glb`, `assets/ak74m.glb`, `assets/ak74u.glb` and `assets/saps12.glb`, which need their sources in `build/pistol9-source`, `build/m1911-source`, `build/beretta-source`, `build/fn502-source`, `build/hipistol-source`, `build/upload-makarov`, `build/upload-ak74m`, `build/upload-krinkov` and `build/upload-saps12`, see Hands and gun), the soldier (see The soldier), his gear and the gloves for the guns' own arms (see Your outfit), then the X4, the MP7A1 and the AR-15 (below), and last the four breakdowns' guns (see Breakdowns). It exports the hand pose and pistol profiles from the game's code, builds and textures the arms in Blender (`tools/blender/build_arms.py`), compresses them, and writes `assets/arms.glb`. It also saves `build/pistol9.blend`, `build/pistol45.blend` and `build/arms.blend`, which you can open in Blender to look at or keep working on the models by hand. `npm run build-pistol`, `npm run build-pistol45`, `npm run build-beretta`, `npm run build-fn502`, `npm run build-hipistol`, `npm run build-makarov`, `npm run build-ak74m`, `npm run build-ak74u`, `npm run build-saps12`, `npm run build-arms`, `npm run build-soldier`, `npm run build-gear`, `npm run build-gun-gloves`, `npm run build-x4`, `npm run build-mp7`, `npm run build-ar15`, `npm run build-breakdown` (the AR-15's), `npm run build-breakdown-scar`, `npm run build-breakdown-galil` and `npm run build-breakdown-pf9` rebuild one at a time (`npm run build-breakdowns` the four breakdowns).

The Xiri X4, the MP7A1 and the AR-15 are built from their downloads (unzipped into `build/upload-xiri`, `build/upload-mp7` and `build/upload-ar15`) onto the soldier's arms, after `assets/soldier.glb` is built; each takes 7 to 18 minutes (`npm run build-x4`, `build-mp7` and `build-ar15` do these). From the `project-fish` folder:

```bash
blender --background --python tools/blender/convert_retarget.py -- build/upload-xiri build/x4-raw.glb --arms assets/soldier.glb
node_modules/.bin/gltf-transform meshopt build/x4-raw.glb assets/x4.glb --level high
blender --background --python tools/blender/convert_retarget.py -- build/upload-mp7 build/mp7-raw.glb --arms assets/soldier.glb
node_modules/.bin/gltf-transform meshopt build/mp7-raw.glb assets/mp7.glb --level high
blender --background --python tools/blender/convert_retarget.py -- build/upload-ar15 build/ar15-raw.glb --arms assets/soldier.glb
node_modules/.bin/gltf-transform meshopt build/ar15-raw.glb assets/ar15.glb --level high
```

`--report FILE` writes the points and clip times it measured (the constants in `src/viewmodel/x4.js`, `mp7.js` and `ar15.js`).

If a model made by hand in Blender replaces one of these files, keep the same scale (metres) and orientation (muzzle towards -Z, Y up), and keep the pistol's parts named as they are (`slide`, `barrel`, `frame`, `trigger`, `safety_blade`, `slide_stop`, `extractor`, `magazine` and the magazine's `magazine_round`, `magazine_second_round`, `magazine_follower` and `magazine_rounds_1` to `_6`) so they animate. The slide stop and the barrel turn about fixed points (`SLIDE_STOP` and `BARREL` in `src/viewmodel/viewmodel.js`), not about their own origins, because compressing the file moves those.
